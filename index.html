Notes on a sovereign, EU-hosted customer support AI

Most e-commerce support automation sends customer data through models hosted outside the EU. For a European merchant, that single fact reshapes the architecture. These notes collect what changes when data residency is a hard requirement, not a preference.

Residency first

When inference runs inside the EU and the data never leaves it, GDPR and the AI Act stop being a compliance afterthought and start shaping the system. Hosting in France, on European infrastructure, removes the cross-border transfer question. The model choice matters here too: an EU model such as Mistral keeps the weights and the inference on the same side of the border, so there is no vendor round trip to reason about.

  • No cross-border transfer agreements to negotiate and keep current.
  • No third-country vendor pulled into scope for every audit.
  • Data stays under the jurisdiction that already regulates it.

Answering is the easy half

A support assistant that only answers questions is a search box with better manners. The useful half is acting: issuing a refund, cancelling an order, pulling live order status, and handing off to a human when confidence drops. That needs real integrations with the store and the help desk, not a chat widget bolted on top. The value shows up when the assistant closes the loop on a request instead of describing how the shopper could close it themselves.

  • Refund or partial refund on a verified order.
  • Cancel or change an order before it ships.
  • Live order and shipping status, pulled in real time.
  • Clean handoff to a human, with the full context attached.

Knowing when to stop

The line between a helpful assistant and a liability is the escalation rule. A sovereign setup logs every decision, exposes a confidence signal, and hands the conversation to a human the moment it is unsure or the request touches money it should not move on its own. Auditability belongs in the design from the start, because it is what makes the system defensible in front of a shopper, a merchant, and a regulator.

Where it meets the help desk

Teams looking at this usually already run a SaaS help desk and want to know what a European, self-hosted setup gives up and what it gains. A direct comparison helps more than a manifesto, so here is a factual Intercom alternative comparison that lays out the trade. The short version: you swap a fully managed non-EU stack for control over where data lives and what the assistant is allowed to do on its own.

What to measure

Deflection rate on its own is a vanity number. The metrics that hold up are the ones tied to a resolved request:

  • Requests resolved without a human touch, not sessions deflected.
  • Time to first useful action, not time to first reply.
  • Share of escalations a human later agreed were correct.

When an assistant escalates well, trust in it compounds, and the automated share can grow without anyone feeling handled by a machine.
